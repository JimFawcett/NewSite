Workload Distribution

AI is not the biggest consumer of Data Center capacity, but it is the second. Most of that is building models. Your use of ChatGPT to ask a dozen questions would use much less than streaming a single NetFlix movie or making an Amazon purchase. You don't have to feel guilty about that ChatGPT use.
Workload Distribution
CategoryShareWhat runs there
Enterprise and business IT~25%Company systems for accounting, sales, email, and internal applications; the largest category, and shrinking in relative terms as companies move to the cloud
AI and machine learning~18%Training large models and answering queries; the fastest-growing category, up from about 10% in 2022
Video streaming~15%Netflix, YouTube, TikTok, Disney+; mostly storage and format conversion at scale
Social media~8%Feed ranking, content storage, ad targeting, and notifications
E-commerce~6%Transactions, inventory, logistics, recommendations, and fraud detection
Search engines~5%Crawling the web, updating the index, and answering queries quickly
Financial services~5%Trading, fraud detection, payment processing, and regulatory reporting
Communications~4%Email, video conferencing, voice calls, and messaging
Gaming~4%Game servers, cloud gaming, and esports infrastructure
Scientific computing~3%Climate simulation, genomics, drug discovery, and particle physics
Government and defense~3%Intelligence analysis, logistics, satellite data, and classified facilities
Healthcare IT~2%Health records, medical imaging archives, genomic databases, and clinical AI
Cybersecurity~1%Threat monitoring, malware analysis, and security operations
Other~1%Connected-device backends, naming and certificate infrastructure, and content delivery

Geographic Distribution

As expected, the US, EU, and China have similar data center capacities. What is surprising is how impoverished Russia is.
Geographic Distribution
RegionShareKey concentrations
United States~35%Northern Virginia (Ashburn) is the largest data center market on earth. Other clusters: Dallas-Fort Worth, Silicon Valley, Chicago, Phoenix, Atlanta, Seattle
European Union and UK~22%Amsterdam, Frankfurt, Dublin, London, and Paris form the FLAP-D cluster; the Nordics draw operators with cold climate and renewable power
China~15%Beijing, Shanghai, Shenzhen, Guangzhou; the Eastern Data, Western Computing plan moves new capacity to Guizhou and Inner Mongolia
India and Australia~7%Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Pune form the fastest-growing major market; Sydney and Melbourne serve the Pacific
Southeast Asia~6%Singapore is the hub; land and power limits push growth to Johor Bahru, Jakarta, Bangkok, and Ho Chi Minh City
Rest of world~6%UAE and Saudi Arabia are investing in AI-scale campuses; also South Africa, Japan, South Korea
Canada and Mexico~4%Toronto, Montreal (cheap hydro), Vancouver; Queretaro, Mexico City, Monterrey
Central and South America~3%Brazil (São Paulo) dominates, with growth in Chile, Colombia, and Argentina
Russia~2%Moscow dominates; law requires Russian user data to stay on Russian soil

Capacity Evolution

Unsustainable growth has to level off, perhaps to be augmented by increasingly powerful servers.
